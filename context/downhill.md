# Downhill preparation — route plan for the long runs

Written 05-08-2026, after reviewing the 2025 NYC marathon lap file
(02-11-2025, 4:48:58). Read by `/daily` and `/week` alongside the rest of
`context/`.

Tagged in the same convention as `equivalence.md`:

- **[CONVENTION]** — established coaching practice, not a controlled study.
- **[INFERENCE]** — my reasoning, flagged as such.
- **[VERIFY]** — geography I believe to be right but have not surveyed. Check

## The problem is distribution, not volume

**[EVIDENCE]** The athlete's long runs already carry as much vertical per mile
as the race does:

| Run | Distance | Gain | ft/mile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 05-08-2026 | 12.5 mi | 627 ft | 50 |
| 31-07-2026 | 10.5 mi | 486 ft | 46 |
| 19-07-2026 | 11.1 mi | 833 ft | 75 |
| **NYC course** | 26.2 mi | 1,496 ft | **57** |

So "add more hills" is the wrong prescription. **[EVIDENCE]** The race delivers
its descent in a few long unbroken chunks — the lap file shows **210 ft of
descent inside mile 2 alone**, off the Verrazzano — where the Central Park loop
delivers the same total in twenty short ones.

**[CONVENTION]** Continuous descent is what accumulates eccentric quadriceps
damage; short rollers allow partial recovery between them. **[INFERENCE]** This
is why the 2025 peak 20-miler on the Hudson greenway (essentially flat) left
the quads unprepared despite a season of otherwise rolling training.

**The metric to progress is therefore the number of *continuous* descents of
≥100 ft, not total elevation.**

## Technique

**[CONVENTION]** Short, quick strides; high cadence; let gravity do the work;
do not reach out in front and brake. **[INFERENCE]** This reduces both peak
braking force on the quad and tibial impact, so it serves the shin history and
the quad goal at once — the athlete reported 05-08-2026 that short strides
already reliably reduce shin discomfort on descents, which is consistent.

**[EVIDENCE]** Note the contrast with what fatigue produced in the race:
cadence *rose* to 166-169 spm over the closing miles while stride length fell
0.966m → 0.845m. High cadence with a collapsing stride is not the same thing as
choosing a short quick stride, and the digest's decay note now distinguishes
them.

## Dosing

**[CONVENTION]** Downhill soreness peaks 24-48 h later and is disproportionate
to how easy it felt at the time. Introduce in small doses; do not judge a
session by how it felt during it.

## Locations, from Central Park

**[VERIFY]** on all of these — approximate figures from general knowledge of
the geography, not a survey.

- **Harlem Hill**, north end of the park loop. The park's biggest, roughly
  80–100 ft. In-park and repeatable, so it is the tool for weeks 3–4 and for
  the gentle week-10 re-entry. The north loop above the 102nd transverse is
  about 1.7 mi, which makes repeats easy to bolt onto a full loop.
- **Cat Hill**, east side around 74th St, roughly 60 ft. Useful for volume, too
  short to count toward the ≥100 ft target.
- **Morningside Drive / Morningside Park escarpment**, 110th–123rd, just west
  of the park's NW corner. A genuinely steep sustained drop into Harlem and
  about the closest thing in Manhattan to a real continuous descent. This is
  the week-5 "one ≥150 ft".
- **Riverside Drive**, on a bluff above the greenway, with several long ramps
  down. Best for stringing multiple sustained descents together in one run.
- **Fort Tryon / Washington Heights**, around 190th. The largest sustained
  grades in Manhattan, but roughly 7 mi north of the park — only worth it on a
  16+ mile day.
- **Queensboro Bridge**, about 1.5 mi from the park's SE corner. The
  Manhattan-side ramp down onto First Ave is the exact feature that preceded
  the 2025 collapse; worth running before race day for the legs and the mental
  map both.
- **Hudson River greenway — avoid for long runs.** Flat. This is where the 2025
  peak 20 was run.

## The course rehearsal (weeks 6 and 11)

**[VERIFY]** distances; map before running.

Central Park → Queensboro Bridge (~2.5 mi) → across and back (~2 mi) → First
Avenue north to the Willis Ave Bridge (~3.5 mi) → Bronx and back over the
Madison Ave Bridge (~1.5 mi) → Fifth Avenue south into the park (~4 mi).

That is roughly 13–14 miles covering the race's last 11, including every
sustained descent in the back half. Make up the balance to 18 or 20 with park
loops **before** starting the course section, so the course miles land on
already-tired legs — which is the whole point.

**[INFERENCE]** The mental rehearsal matters as much as the eccentric loading
here. The athlete reported 05-08-2026 that the long straight northward stretch
of First Avenue, knowing the Bronx and the turnaround were still ahead, is
where the 2025 race came apart both physically and mentally. Running it in
training removes the surprise.
